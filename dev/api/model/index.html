Parameters and settings

AshDieback.AshDiebackParameters — Type
AshDiebackParameters(; r_symp, r_allee, beta_0, beta_1, D, S_sat, gamma, C_pers, C_init,
                     kappa)
AshDiebackParameters(v::AbstractVector)

The ten model parameters, in PEOC's order. It is an AbstractVector, so θ[5] == θ.D and collect(θ) gives the vector that PEOC saves.

indexfieldpapermeaning
1r_sympr_Sscale of the symptom probability
2r_alleerAllee threshold of spore production
3beta_0β₀baseline spore production
4beta_1β₁effect of June rainfall on spore production
5DDdiffusion coefficient (km² day⁻¹)
6S_satSsaturation of the spore load
7gammaγtemperature index that stops symptoms
8C_persC_persprobability that symptoms persist (C_non_recover)
9C_initC_initscale of the imputed initial prevalence
10kappaκexponent of the temperature function (puiss)

Scaling (r_symp, r_allee, S_sat) by the same factor leaves the model unchanged, so the paper fixes r_symp = 1000.

AshDieback.ModelSettings — Type
ModelSettings(; initialization_year = 2007, final_year = 2019, observed_trees = 30,
              delta_t = 0.1, diffusion_period = 60.0, cap_symptom_probability = false)

Settings of the model that are not estimated (PEOC's parameters_of_the_model.py).

  • initialization_year: the first of the two years whose colonised rachis is imputed; diffusion is simulated from initialization_year + 2.
  • final_year: the last survey year in the likelihood (2019 in the paper; later years are kept for validation).
  • observed_trees: trees observed per plot visit (m = 30).
  • delta_t, diffusion_period: time step and duration τ (days) of the spore diffusion.
  • cap_symptom_probability: cap the yearly probability of developing symptoms at 1, as in eq. (6) of the paper. PEOC does not, which gives a NaN likelihood when it exceeds 1; false reproduces PEOC.

AshDieback.temperature_effect — Function
temperature_effect(T, gamma, kappa)

The temperature function f(T) = ([1 − T/γ]₊)^κ of the symptom model (eq. 7 of the paper): symptoms become rarer as summers get hotter and stop at T ≥ γ. Follows IEEE powers: 0^κ = 0 for κ > 0 and 0^0 = 1.

Problem and solution

AshDieback.AshDiebackProblem — Type
AshDiebackProblem(data::PEOCData, p; settings = ModelSettings())

The mechanistic-statistical ash dieback model of Fritsch et al. on data, with parameters p (an AshDiebackParameters or a vector in its order).

solve(prob) simulates the colonised rachis and the probability of symptoms in every quadrat (an AshDiebackSolution); loglikelihood(sol) scores it against the survey. remake(prob; p = θ) changes the parameters and reuses everything else.

source
CommonSolve.solve — Function
solve(prob::AshDiebackProblem, alg = SparseCrankNicolson())

Simulate the model: impute the first two years, then for each later year produce spores from the rachis of the two previous years (with the Allee effect and rainfall), diffuse them with alg (an AbstractDiffusionAlgorithm) and saturate them at S_sat; finally compute the probability of symptoms for each survey year.

source
solve(prob::HostProblem, alg = SparseCrankNicolson())

Simulate the spore model and the host together. The spore source of year a becomes β (χ{a−2} + χ{a−1}) with χ = F × (live host weighted by its inoculum), then diffusion and the cap at S as in the paper.

source
SciMLBase.remake — Method
remake(prob::AshDiebackProblem; p = prob.p, settings = prob.settings)

A copy of prob with parameters p and settings. With the same settings, the data and precomputed constants are shared. Changing final_year extends the simulation beyond the fitted period, as in the paper's validation on 2020–2023: remake(prob; settings = ModelSettings(; final_year = 2023)).

AshDieback.initial_fungi — Function
initial_fungi(prob::AshDiebackProblem)

The imputed colonised rachis per ash of the two years before the first simulated one, obtained by inverting the symptom model with q ≈ Cinit Ψ (PEOC's `relativefongi_1/2`). Where f(T) = 0 it is replaced by 1 before dividing.

source
AshDieback.symptom_probability — Function
symptom_probability(prob::AshDiebackProblem, fungi)

The probability qa that a tree has symptoms in each survey year, from the colonised rachis per ash fungi of the year before: q̃a = F{a−1} f(T{a−1}) / rS of developing them, and qa = q̃a (1 − Cpers q{a−1}) + Cpers q_{a−1} with q = 0 before the first survey year.

A changing host

AshDieback.HostClass — Type
HostClass(name; share = 1.0, susceptibility = 1.0, mortality = 0.0, inoculum = 1.0,
          offspring = name)

One kind of ash in a HostDynamics.

  • share: its share of the host at the start, and of recruits not inheriting from a parent.
  • susceptibility: s, multiplying the yearly chance of developing symptoms.
  • mortality: μ, the yearly chance that a tree with symptoms dies of the disease.
  • inoculum: c, the infected rachis it produces, relative to an ordinary tree.
  • offspring: the class of the recruits inheriting from it. A class made by a treatment names the untreated class here, as a vaccinated tree's seedlings are not vaccinated.
source
AshDieback.HostDynamics — Type
HostDynamics(classes; background_mortality = 0.0, juvenile_years = 1.0,
             juvenile_mortality_factor = 1.0, juvenile_inoculum = 0.0,
             regeneration_rate = 0.0, heritability = 0.0)

How the ash itself changes from year to year, around the spore model of the paper. The host in each quadrat is divided into classes (HostClass), each with adults and juveniles, healthy or with symptoms. Each year, in this order:

  1. Symptoms: with q̃ = F f(T)/rS as in the paper, a tree at risk develops them with chance min(1, s q̃). Trees at risk are the healthy ones and the share 1 − Cpers that recover.
  2. Death: trees with symptoms die of the disease with chance μ, multiplied by juvenile_mortality_factor for juveniles and capped at 1, then all trees die with chance background_mortality.
  3. Maturation: a share 1/juvenile_years of surviving juveniles become adults.
  4. Recruitment: healthy juveniles are added at a rate (A/A₀) m N + ρ A max(0, 1 − N/K), where A is adult area, N all live area and m the background mortality, all before this year's changes, ρ is regeneration_rate, and K and A₀ are the starting live and adult area of the quadrat. The first term replaces background deaths, so an undiseased stand stays as it started, and the second fills gaps left by the disease. A share heritability of recruits follow their parents' classes, by adult area, and the rest follow each class's share.
  5. Interventions (see AbstractIntervention).

Live trees produce infected rachis in proportion to c, or c times juvenile_inoculum for juveniles, so that χ = F Σ c (area) replaces χ = F d of the paper. Juveniles start at the share that an undiseased stand keeps. constant_host gives the paper's model.

AshDieback.HostProblem — Type
HostProblem(prob::AshDiebackProblem, host::HostDynamics; interventions = [])

The model prob with the changing host host in place of its constant ash density, and any AbstractInterventions. Solve it with solve, which gives a HostSolution. The data of prob supply the starting host (ash_density), the climate and the imputed first two years, so a future climate is given by building prob with a longer climate array and a later final_year.

AshDieback.HostSolution — Type
HostSolution

The result of solve(::HostProblem).

Fields, with the host in the units of ash_density and years given by host_years:

  • fungi: colonised rachis per ash F, size (nx, ny, years - 1), for the simulated_years. F of year a drives the symptoms of year a + 1.
  • healthy, symptomatic: adults, size (nx, ny, classes, years).
  • juvenile_healthy, juvenile_symptomatic: juveniles, of the same size.
  • disease_deaths: cumulative area killed by the disease, size (nx, ny, years).
  • applied: the area each intervention acted on, size (interventions, years): trees vaccinated, planted or felled, or adults in quadrats whose litter was treated.
  • prob, alg: the HostProblem and the diffusion algorithm.

Interventions

AshDieback.AbstractIntervention — Type
AbstractIntervention

Supertype of what can be done to the host: Vaccinate, PlantStock, Fell and ReduceLitter. Interventions apply in a HostProblem after the year's changes to the host and before its rachis is counted, in the order given. Each has years, when it acts, and cells, a Bool matrix of the quadrats it acts on (nothing for all), which is how targeting by region or host density is expressed. The area each one acts on per year is in HostSolution.applied.

source
AshDieback.Vaccinate — Type
Vaccinate(; from, to, years, rate, max_coverage = 1.0, waning = 0.0, juveniles = true,
          symptomatic = false, cells = nothing)

Each year in years, move a share rate of the eligible trees of class from to class to (usually a vaccinated_class), until to holds a share max_coverage of the two together. Healthy trees are eligible, with juveniles if juveniles, and trees with symptoms if symptomatic, which stay symptomatic but take on the mortality of to.

Protection is lost at the yearly rate waning in every year, including after years: a share of to returns to from, in whatever state it is. Repeat doses need no separate setting, since waned trees are eligible again and count towards the area treated.

source
AshDieback.vaccinated_class — Function
vaccinated_class(from::HostClass; efficacy, mortality = from.mortality,
                 inoculum = from.inoculum, name = Symbol(:vaccinated_, from.name))

The class that Vaccinate moves trees of from into. A generic vaccine, with no product assumed: efficacy is ε, so the susceptibility is (1 − ε) s. It starts empty, and its recruits belong to from.

source
AshDieback.PlantStock — Type
PlantStock(; class, years, rate, cells = nothing)

Each year in years, plant healthy juveniles of class, up to a share rate of the quadrat's starting host, without taking live area above that starting host. So planting fills gaps, and its lag before it matters is juvenile_years of the HostDynamics.

source
AshDieback.Fell — Type
Fell(; years, rate, cells = nothing)

Each year in years, remove a share rate of adults with symptoms in every class. Felled area is not counted in disease_deaths.

source
AshDieback.ReduceLitter — Type
ReduceLitter(; years, effect, cells = nothing)

Each year in years, remove a share effect of the year's infected rachis, by clearing or treating leaf litter. It lowers χ of that year, so the spores of the next two years. It does not act on the trees, or on the spores that arrive from elsewhere.

Diffusion

AshDieback.AbstractDiffusionAlgorithm — Type
AbstractDiffusionAlgorithm

Supertype of the methods that solve the spore diffusion step of the model, ∂w/∂t = D Δw + ν/τ with w(0) = 0 and zero-flux boundaries, over the diffusion period τ. Every method advances the same Crank–Nicolson scheme as PEOC: (I + cL) wᵏ⁺¹ = (I − cL) wᵏ + Δt ν/τ with c = DΔt/(2h²), for N = τ/Δt steps.

source
AshDieback.SparseCrankNicolson — Type
SparseCrankNicolson(; linsolve = CHOLMODFactorization())

Crank–Nicolson steps with one sparse factorisation of I + cL per likelihood, solved through LinearSolve.jl with the algorithm linsolve.

I + cL is a symmetric M-matrix and I − cL has no negative entries while c ≤ 1/4 (D ≤ h²/(2Δt), 1280 km² day⁻¹ for 16 km quadrats), so no step cancels and every value of the field keeps its relative accuracy, including the tails down to 1e-134 that the likelihood reads through log(q). A Cholesky factorisation (the default) or an LU with diagonal pivots keeps this property; a transform (FFT/DCT) solver does not.

source
AshDieback.DenseCrankNicolson — Type
DenseCrankNicolson()

The reference Crank–Nicolson implementation of PEOC 1.0 (its 'exact' solver): the dense inverse M = (I + cL)⁻¹, A = 2M − I and b = Δt Mν/τ, then wᵏ⁺¹ = A wᵏ + b. About 30 times slower than SparseCrankNicolson and kept for testing.

source
AshDieback.neumann_laplacian — Function
neumann_laplacian(nx, ny)

The 5-point Laplacian on an nx × ny grid with zero-flux (Neumann) boundaries, as a sparse positive semi-definite matrix L (sign flipped, times h²) acting on vec of an nx × ny array: L = I ⊗ L₁(nx) + L₁(ny) ⊗ I with L₁(m) = tridiag(−1, 2, −1) and corner entries 1. This is the cell-centred, mirror-ghost-cell discretisation that PEOC uses.

source

Likelihood

StatsAPI.loglikelihood — Function
loglikelihood(sol::AshDiebackSolution)
loglikelihood(prob::AshDiebackProblem, alg = SparseCrankNicolson())

The binomial log-likelihood of the survey: the number of trees with symptoms in quadrat i in year a is Binomial(m × visits, q_a^i), independently. It is -Inf when the likelihood is zero and when a probability falls outside [0, 1] (possible because PEOC does not cap the symptom probability; see ModelSettings).

Equal to PEOC's value except in the corner cases listed in peoc_loglikelihood.

source
loglikelihood(post::AshDiebackPosterior, x::AbstractVector)

The log-likelihood at the free parameters x (not checked against the prior).

source
AshDieback.peoc_loglikelihood — Function
peoc_loglikelihood(sol::AshDiebackSolution)

The log-likelihood exactly as PEOC computes it: a pair (ℓ, zero) where zero is true when the likelihood is exactly zero (a quadrat with q = 0 and an infected tree, or q = 1 and a healthy one). PEOC stores ℓ * (1 - zero), so a stored 0 means a zero likelihood.

PEOC's quirks are kept: quadrats with q = 1 reset the flag of the same year instead of adding to it, the sum stops at the first flagged year but keeps its partial value, and a probability outside [0, 1] gives NaN. loglikelihood is the corrected version.

source
AshDieback.BinomialTerms — Type
BinomialTerms

Constants of the binomial likelihood of one survey year: for each visited quadrat, the number of trees with symptoms k = round(n p) (ties to even) and without, n − k, out of n = m × visits, and log_choose = log C(n, k).
